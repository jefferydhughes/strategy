# Introduction

**Safeguarding the Core While Embracing Change**

Kingswood University is dedicated to providing transformative education that nurtures spiritual growth, fosters deep learning, and equips students for meaningful careers. To sustain this mission, the university must continuously evolve and adapt its strategies for teaching, learning, and financial sustainability.

The landscape of Christian higher education today can be likened to a strategic game of Risk. Imagine committing all your resources to expand outward, only to leave your central territories vulnerable. Many Christian universities, including Kingswood, find themselves in a similar position—stretched thin by efforts to address peripheral demands while neglecting the core of their mission.

In the race to attract more students, universities often overextend themselves by launching professional degrees, graduate programs, and sprawling student services. This strategy aims to claim a larger share of a shrinking market, but it often comes at a cost. The outcome? Rising tuition, increased bureaucracy, and diminishing focus on what truly sets Christian education apart. The result is a sector at risk: private Christian universities losing students, hemorrhaging finances, and in some cases, closing their doors permanently.

**A Vicious Cycle**

The measures many institutions adopt to stay afloat often exacerbate the problem:

1. **Lowering Admission Standards:** While this broadens access and diversifies the student population, it often leads to higher dropout rates as students face financial or academic challenges. This, in turn, necessitates increased spending on support services and financial aid, leading to reduced faculty resources.
2. **Expanding Course Offerings:** While diversification may attract more students, it risks spreading resources too thin, forcing smaller universities to compete with larger, better-funded institutions.

These challenges reveal a vicious cycle that jeopardizes the ability of Christian universities to deliver on their mission.

**The Path Forward for Kingswood**

This report outlines strategies to help Kingswood University refocus its resources on core programs while exploring revenue-generating initiatives aligned with its mission and culture. These proposals emphasize strategic analysis, cost-effectiveness, and financial sustainability.

Some revenue opportunities, such as renting campus facilities for corporate events, will have minimal impact on the university's core identity. Others, such as expanding online degree offerings, may require deeper reflection on how they align with Kingswood's mission and long-term vision.

**Key Questions for Consideration**

As Kingswood navigates these decisions, institutional leaders must ask:

- Does this initiative align with our mission and values?
- Will it strengthen or dilute the core identity of Kingswood University?
- Is this the legacy we wish to build for future generations?

Pursuing revenue opportunities without careful consideration risks compromising the university's unique identity. However, when thoughtfully approached, these initiatives can serve as a catalyst for innovation, growth, and renewed purpose.

In the pages ahead, we will explore how Kingswood can embrace change while safeguarding the heart of its mission. The goal is not merely to survive but to thrive as a model of transformative Christian education in an increasingly complex world.

# Background and Overview

Over the past two decades, Canada has experienced notable shifts in religious affiliation, higher education enrollment, charitable giving, and the cost of higher education. Below is an overview of these trends, supported by relevant statistics and sources.

**1\. Decline of Christianity in Canada over the past 20 years**

Canada has experienced a significant decline in Christian affiliation:

- In 2001, 77.1% of Canadians identified as Christian.
- By 2011, this proportion decreased to 67.3%.
- In 2021, it further declined to 53.3%.

Concurrently, the percentage of Canadians reporting no religious affiliation has risen:

- 16.5% in 2001
- 23.9% in 2011
- 34.6% in 2021

This trend indicates a substantial shift towards secularism in Canadian society over the past two decades.

- In 2022, 617,301 students graduated with a certificate, diploma, or degree from a Canadian public postsecondary institution, marking a 2.7% decrease from the previous year.
- This decline was observed in both universities (-2.6%) and colleges (-2.9%).

This downward trend in graduations may reflect broader challenges in higher education enrollment.

# Academic Opportunities to Increase Revenue

Tuition remains one of the largest and most reliable sources of revenue for educational institutions. To strengthen and diversify this stream, Kingswood University should consider adapting its academic offerings to reach untapped audiences and emerging markets. By reimagining how and to whom we deliver education, Kingswood can expand its influence while maintaining its commitment to excellence in Christian education.

**Innovative Program Offerings**

1. **Degree Completion Programs for Pastors:** Many ministry professionals lack the time or resources to complete traditional degree programs. Flexible degree completion pathways, tailored specifically for pastors and ministry leaders, can fill this gap. Institutions like Indiana Wesleyan University have successfully launched similar programs, demonstrating the demand for such initiatives.
2. **Community College Pathways:** Building partnerships with community colleges can create seamless transfer pathways for students to continue their education at Kingswood. These pathways can be particularly appealing for students seeking a Christian education after completing foundational courses at a lower cost.
3. **Accelerated Three-Year Degree Programs:** Accelerated programs allow students to complete their education in less time, reducing costs and preparing them for their careers sooner. Institutions like Gordon College and Southern Wesleyan University have implemented successful three-year degree tracks to attract cost-conscious students.
4. **Promote Online Learning Opportunities:** Online education provides unparalleled flexibility for learners worldwide. By expanding Kingswood's online offerings, the university can reach new markets, including international students and adult learners. Liberty University is a prime example of how online learning can transform enrollment and revenue.
5. **Micro-Credentialing and Stackable Certifications:** Offering stackable microcredentials and certifications in areas like ministry leadership, counseling, and theology can attract working professionals looking to upskill or diversify their qualifications. These programs can be delivered online, in person, or integrated into existing degree pathways, providing flexibility for learners.

1. **Workshops, Symposia, and Intensives:** Developing short-term training programs, such as weeklong intensive courses, coaching sessions, and symposiums, can appeal to both current students and professionals seeking skill enhancement. For example, Wheaton College offers a range of theological workshops that draw participants from across the country.
2. **Entrepreneurial Programs and Learning Labs:** Launching entrepreneurial-focused initiatives that align with Kingswood's mission, such as church planting labs or nonprofit management workshops, can attract a new demographic of learners passionate about social impact and leadership. These programs can be delivered remotely in partnership with local churches.
3. **Mastermind Classes and Conferences:** Hosting mastermind groups and conferences led by experienced faculty or industry leaders can generate revenue while fostering community engagement. Topics could range from biblical studies to innovative ministry practices.

**Global and International Expansion**

Kingswood has the opportunity to expand its reach to new markets, particularly internationally. By offering online and hybrid learning options tailored to global audiences, Kingswood can establish a presence in regions seeking high-quality Christian education. Partnering with international churches or missionary organizations can further enhance these efforts.

Another venue to explore is hosting summer camps for international experiences. Kingswood can partner with organizations that already run these programs, or build their own.

**Graduate and Professional Programs**

Creating stackable graduate programs designed for working professionals can open doors for individuals seeking advanced education without committing to full-time studies. # Auxiliary Services and Facility Rentals: Maximizing Campus Resources

Like strategic partnerships, auxiliary services and campus facilities represent untapped revenue opportunities for Kingswood University. By optimizing the use of campus spaces and exploring innovative ways to generate income, Kingswood can transform underutilized resources into valuable assets while enhancing student experiences.

### **Inventory and Optimization of Campus Spaces**

Kingswood should conduct a comprehensive inventory of its campus facilities to identify underutilized spaces and land. Key steps to maximize efficiency include:

- **Dispose of Surplus Land or Facilities:** Selling or leasing unused properties can free up resources for reinvestment in core operations.
- **Consolidate and Repurpose Administrative Spaces:** Combining departments or redesigning existing spaces can reduce maintenance costs and create more functional environments.
- **Adopt Remote Work Policies:** Allowing administrative staff to work remotely when feasible can reduce the demand for office space, freeing areas for other purposes or rentals.

### **Facility and Property Management**

Evaluating the potential benefits of outsourcing facility and property management could lead to significant cost savings and improved operational efficiency. Partnering with experienced firms can ensure professional oversight while reducing Kingswood's direct management responsibilities.

### **Summer Rental Opportunities**

Kingswood's campus offers significant potential for summer rentals. Developing a structured plan to rent out every available bed during the summer months can provide consistent seasonal revenue. Possible rental opportunities include:

- **Conference and Event Hosting:** Attracting organizations for retreats, training sessions, or ministry events.

To enhance the appeal of these rentals, Kingswood can offer additional services, such as:

- **Catering and Event Planning:** Providing professional event services to renters, which could generate supplemental income.
- **Operations and Equipment Rentals:** Offering access to audio-visual equipment, furniture, or other resources for events held on campus.

### **Student Work-Study Opportunities**

Auxiliary services can also be designed to provide hands-on learning experiences for students. Programs could include:

- **Catering and Event Management Training:** Giving students real-world skills by involving them in the planning and execution of campus events.
- **Facilities Management Internships:** Partnering with property management companies to create student internships in maintenance, hospitality, or operational logistics.

### **A Sustainable Approach**

By thoughtfully managing its auxiliary services and facility rentals, Kingswood can unlock new revenue streams while ensuring that its campus resources are used to their fullest potential. These efforts not only enhance financial stability but also provide students with valuable opportunities for growth and professional development.

## Hotel Partnerships

Shared ownership of a hotel can generate revenue while also providing field experience for students in relevant academic programs. In 2012, Metropolitan State University – Denver opened a SpringHill Suites by Marriott hotel in downtown Denver in partnership with development group [Sage Hospitality](https://sagehospitalitygroup.com/). The hotel site also includes a Hotel and

Hospitality Learning Center for MSUD's Hospitality, Tourism, and Events students. Students get hands-on experience by working in the hotel while completing their degree, and "industry experts" are employed as adjunct faculty members. This endeavor has been very financially successful.

"The revenue is paying for the debt on the academic space, and \[in 2017\], the corporation overseeing the hotel turned over \$2 million to the university foundation – with half

being used for scholarships and the other half being used to further improve the hospitality program."

Also in 2012, the University of Wisconsin – Oshkosh partnered with two local hoteliers to purchase the [City Center Hotel](https://www.uwosh.edu/today/16905/downtown-oshkosh-hotel-officially-under-new-ownership/). The hotel provides space for UWO's academic conferences, and may house a hospitality program in the future.

## Retirement Communities

For example, the [Kendal Retirement Community at Oberlin College](https://kao.kendal.org/) houses over 300

residents who are allowed to audit classes at Oberlin free of charge. Another example is The [Forest at Duke University](https://forestduke.org/), a retirement community that has housed residents since 1992.

With this model, Kingswood can create a single entry point for all students. The stackable nature of our programs allows students to easily enter and exit the programs as their time allows. They believe in the philosophy of learning a little, using it a lot, and then upskilling when needed.

# Conclusion: Charting a Sustainable and Impactful Future for Kingswood University

Kingswood University stands at a pivotal moment in its history, where challenges and opportunities converge. The decline of Christianity, rising costs, and shifting higher education landscapes necessitate a bold and strategic approach to securing the university's mission and future. By diversifying revenue streams, fostering innovative partnerships, and reimagining the use of campus resources, Kingswood can thrive as a transformative institution that prepares students for meaningful lives and careers.

A Unified Vision for Revenue Generation

The proposed initiatives outlined in this report are designed to create both immediate and long-term revenue solutions while aligning with Kingswood's mission and values. These include:

1. **Academic Opportunities:** Expanding program offerings, focusing on retention, micro-credentialing, and leveraging international markets to attract and support new student populations.
2. **Focus on Uniqueness and Innovation:** Kingswood should prioritize offering programs that set it apart from other institutions by leveraging its strengths and cultural context. By focusing on areas where Kingswood can excel and deliver better than anyone else, the university can create a distinctive identity. Additionally, developing innovative programs that are not available anywhere else in the country will attract students seeking unique educational opportunities. These programs should reflect Kingswood's values, location, and mission, ensuring they are both relevant and compelling to prospective students.
3. **Strategic Partnerships:** Collaborating with event coordinators, property developers, and other institutions to maximize land use, share resources, and open new streams of revenue.
4. **Auxiliary Services and Facility Rentals:** Optimizing campus spaces for summer rentals, conferences, and events while providing students with valuable hands-on learning opportunities.
5. **Innovative Revenue Streams:** Pursuing large-scale ventures such as acquiring businesses, developing mixed-use spaces, and establishing a non-profit business development organization to ensure sustainable financial growth.

Strengthening the Core Mission

While revenue generation is crucial, these strategies are designed to bolster Kingswood's core mission of academic and spiritual development. By reducing dependency on student fees, Kingswood can provide more accessible education, invest in academic excellence, and empower students to thrive in a changing world.

Achieving Financial Independence

The five-year goal of replacing tuition revenue for 300 students is both ambitious and attainable. Achieving this target will require the commitment of institutional leaders to make bold decisions, engage with external partners, and embrace innovative solutions. With \$6 million in annual recurring revenue, Kingswood can build an endowment to ensure long-term stability while continuing to serve its students and community with excellence.

A Legacy of Leadership and Vision

Kingswood University has a unique opportunity to redefine what it means to be a mission-driven institution in the 21st century. By embracing innovation, leveraging its resources, and staying true to its values, Kingswood can create a sustainable and impactful future that leaves a lasting legacy for generations to come.
