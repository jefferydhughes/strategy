# Program Overview

This document presents two redesigned, sequenced first-year courses that together form the foundation of Kingswood University's Outdoor Adventure offering. The pair replaces the prior single-course format with a coherent two-semester arc that balances field competence with academic rigor.

## Design Rationale

The original program delivered valuable experiences but lacked structural coherence. This redesign tightens the learning sequence around three principles:

1. Front-load weather-dependent field experiences. The fall backpacking expedition is scheduled in weeks three to four of Adventure I, before regional weather deteriorates.
2. Use the long Maritime winter as a strategic asset. Adventure II is built around classroom-based theory — risk management, expedition planning, outdoor leadership, ecology, and sustainable tourism — punctuated by applied winter field components.
3. Build progressively from skills to leadership. Adventure I produces competent expedition members. Adventure II produces emerging expedition leaders.

## Assessment Model (Both Courses)

Consistent across both courses to allow students to develop and demonstrate growth in the same four dimensions:

- Skills Demonstrations — 30%
- Reflective Journal — 20%
- Expedition Planning Project — 30%
- Participation and Field Conduct — 20%

The expedition planning project is tuned differently in each course: Adventure I requires a personal multi-day trip plan; Adventure II requires a full risk-managed expedition proposal with leadership rationale, environmental impact assessment, and budget.

## Calendar Description

An introduction to wilderness travel and outdoor living in the Maritime environment. Students develop core competencies in backcountry travel, navigation, camp craft, bushcraft, and group safety through a mix of classroom instruction and field experience, including a multi-day backpacking expedition and certification in Wilderness First Aid.

## Extended Course Description

Outdoor Adventure I is the entry point into Kingswood's outdoor adventure program. The course is designed to take students with limited or no backcountry experience to a level of competence where they can travel and camp safely in the Maritime wilderness as informed group members. The course front-loads the signature field experience — a three-day, two-night backpacking expedition — in the early weeks of the semester to take advantage of stable fall weather. From that foundation, students build outward into navigation, shelter, fire craft, water and nutrition, paddling fundamentals, and emergency response. The semester concludes with the Wilderness First Aid certification weekend and a personal expedition planning project.

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. Demonstrate safe and competent backcountry travel and camping skills during a multi-day expedition in variable Maritime conditions.
2. Use map, compass, and basic GPS techniques to navigate on-trail and off-trail terrain.
3. Apply Leave No Trace principles in all field environments and articulate the ethical reasoning behind them.
4. Select, pack, use, and maintain appropriate personal and group equipment for three-season wilderness travel.
5. Demonstrate fundamental bushcraft and wilderness self-reliance skills, including fire craft, shelter construction, water sourcing, and basic survival priorities.
6. Function effectively as a member of a small expedition team, contributing to group safety, decision-making, and morale.
7. Hold a current Wilderness First Aid certification from the Outdoor Council of Canada.
8. Plan a personal multi-day wilderness trip including route, gear, nutrition, risk assessment, and emergency response considerations.

## Calendar Description

A theory-led second course in the Outdoor Adventure sequence focused on risk management, expedition planning, outdoor leadership, ecology, and sustainable tourism. Applied components include winter camping, snow shelter construction (conditions permitting), and downhill skiing. Students prepare a full expedition proposal as the culminating project.

## Extended Course Description

Outdoor Adventure II is built around the recognition that a Maritime winter is an asset, not a limitation. The course uses the classroom as its primary instructional environment to develop the planning, decision-making, and leadership frameworks that distinguish an experienced expedition member from a competent trip leader. Applied learning takes the form of a winter expedition intensive that includes camping on the campus sports fields, snow shelter construction when snowpack allows, and two days of downhill skiing as a controlled environment for cold-weather mobility and group management. The semester concludes with a full expedition proposal that integrates the four academic pillars of the course.

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. Apply structured risk management frameworks to plan and lead outdoor activities, including hazard identification, risk assessment, and mitigation strategies.
2. Develop a complete expedition plan covering logistics, route, gear, food, emergency response, communication, and budget.
3. Compare and apply established outdoor leadership models in scenarios involving group decision-making, conflict, and emergent risk.
4. Demonstrate competence in winter camping, including shelter selection, cold-weather sleep systems, nutrition and hydration, and avoidance of cold injuries.
5. Analyze the ecological context of outdoor recreation in the Maritime region and articulate principles of sustainable wilderness use.
6. Evaluate sustainable tourism models and their implications for outdoor program design and Canadian destination communities.
7. Demonstrate emerging leadership capacity through field role rotation, peer feedback, and self-assessment.

# Appendix A — Future Certification Pathway

The following third-party certifications are not embedded in either course but represent a recommended pathway for students who wish to extend their qualifications. As the program matures, selected certifications could be embedded or offered as co-curricular weekends, similar to the current Wilderness First Aid arrangement.

- Paddle Canada — Introductory Canoe or Sea Kayak (open water).
- Leave No Trace Canada — Trainer Course (16 hours).
- Avalanche Skills Training 1 (Avalanche Canada) — for students pursuing winter mountain travel.
- Wilderness First Responder (Outdoor Council of Canada) — as an upgrade from WFA.
- Swiftwater Rescue Technician — for students pursuing river-based recreation.

# Appendix B — Field Component and Risk Considerations

Both courses involve field activity in variable conditions. The following standing items should be confirmed for each cohort:

- Medical clearance and personal readiness questionnaires on file for all students prior to any field component.
- Trip plans filed with department administration prior to all expeditions, including communication schedule and emergency contacts.
- Instructor-to-student ratios consistent with Outdoor Council of Canada and institutional risk policy.
- Documented incident reporting and review process with post-incident debrief protocol.
- Insurance and liability waivers signed in advance of field experiences.
- Conditions-based authority for the instructor to modify or cancel any field component.
