# Kingswood University Business Development Prioritization

**Prepared for:** Kingswood University leadership  
**Prepared by:** Jeff Hughes, working from a business-development consultant perspective  
**Planning horizon:** 2026-2031  
**Status:** Working strategic hypothesis; not an approved institutional plan  
**Last updated:** September 14, 2026

## 1. Governing recommendation

Kingswood should concentrate near-term business-development capacity on revenue-producing initiatives that use assets it already owns, improve the contribution generated by current programs, create meaningful work for students, and reduce long-term dependence on tuition.

The recommended sequence is:

1. **Improve the economics of what Kingswood already operates.** Grow MBA and Business enrolment, develop employer and church channels, and build a reliable conversion system.
2. **Monetize underused assets with low-capital pilots.** Begin with summer accommodation, conferences, retreats, facility rentals, camps, and short-form education.
3. **Build sponsored platforms combining mission, learning, work, and revenue.** Pursue the Business Lab, naming rights, employer-sponsored projects, student enterprises, and community partnerships.
4. **Use evidence and revenue from these pilots to support the 2031 institutional model.** Explore a distinct School of Business, experiential academic frameworks, and purpose-built facilities only after demand, economics, governance, and accreditation pathways are established.

The Spring 2027 Amplify Summit is no longer a near-term priority. **Amplify moves to 2028 or later** and should be developed as part of the wider Kingswood School of Business portfolio.

## 2. Kingswood in 2031

By 2031, Kingswood can operate as a mission-driven Christian university in which education, enterprise, community engagement, and student formation reinforce one another.

- Tuition remains important but is not the only engine supporting education.
- Residences, classrooms, recreation areas, kitchens, fields, and future facilities generate year-round revenue.
- Students gain paid work, practicums, client projects, venture experience, and leadership development through campus enterprises and partners.
- Employers, churches, nonprofits, donors, tourism operators, and entrepreneurs participate as customers, sponsors, mentors, referral partners, and project partners.
- The School of Business has a distinct market identity and a Christian liberal-arts orientation while remaining aligned with Kingswood's mission.
- Programs form clear, flexible pathways rather than operating as isolated credentials.
- New buildings are designed as productive assets with academic, student-life, community, and revenue functions.
- A strategic growth and partnerships function owns opportunity validation, business cases, partnerships, launches, measurement, and improvement.

### Structural hypothesis requiring formal study

Kingswood's Bible-college accreditation requires a substantial Bible-credit component within each major. A more distinct School of Business may create room for a Christian liberal-arts business education with a different academic balance.

This is **not yet a legal, governance, or accreditation conclusion**. It requires analysis of degree-granting authority, accreditation, governance, legal structure, curriculum, faculty, administrative cost, student aid, brand architecture, and mission alignment.

The decision test is whether a distinct structure would improve student demand, academic quality, mission impact, and financial sustainability enough to justify the complexity.

## 3. Business-development mandate

### Business development should lead

- Revenue opportunity identification and validation
- Market research and customer discovery
- Partnerships, sponsorships, and naming rights
- Business cases and financial models
- Campus-asset utilization
- Employer, church, nonprofit, and tourism channels
- Executive education and non-degree offers
- Recruitment-funnel optimization with Admissions and Marketing
- Cross-functional pilots and performance dashboards

### Business development should co-lead

- Academic-program market validation
- Student employment and enterprise models
- Experiential-learning partnerships
- Events combining recruitment, learning, community, and revenue
- Revenue-producing capital projects
- School of Business brand and structural development

### Business development should not own

- Academic standards, curriculum approval, or faculty governance
- Accreditation decisions
- Spiritual formation or student assessment
- Advancement relationships without agreed coordination
- Facilities operations after a venture becomes routine

The proposed role should create and validate opportunities, assemble partners, build business cases, coordinate launches, and measure results without bypassing academic or institutional authority.

## 8. Proposed 2031 organizational direction

### President

- **Academic Affairs:** Faculty, academic quality, Bible and Ministry programs, School of Business academic leadership, approval, and accreditation
- **Strategic Growth & Partnerships:** Opportunity portfolio, partnerships, campus ventures, earned revenue, business cases, cross-functional launches, and scorecard
- **Enrolment & Marketing:** Brand, lead generation, admissions, and conversion
- **Advancement:** Donors, major gifts, alumni, and capital relationships
- **Student Experience:** Formation, residence life, retention, activities, and student-employment partnership
- **Finance & Operations:** Finance, facilities, hospitality operations, risk, and enterprise delivery

### Future School of Business portfolio

- Undergraduate Business
- MBA and graduate leadership
- Executive education and micro-credentials
- Business Lab and employer projects
- Entrepreneurship and marketplace-ministry programming
- Student enterprise and work-integrated learning
- Amplify, when justified in 2028 or later

Academic authority remains with academic governance. Business development validates markets, develops partners, builds business cases, and coordinates growth across boundaries.

## 9. First 90-day business-development agenda

### Days 1-30: Establish facts and select pilots

- Verify enrolment, tuition, retention, conversion, program contribution, and capacity.
- Inventory rentable assets, available dates, restrictions, and utilization.
- Interview leaders in Academics, Finance, Facilities, Admissions, Marketing, Advancement, and Student Life.
- Validate accommodation, retreats, rentals, executive education, and employer partnerships.
- Select no more than three pilots.

### Days 31-60: Build and approve business cases

- Model customer, offer, price, volume, revenue, costs, contribution, capacity, risk, owner, and KPIs.
- Define approval and decision rights.
- Secure operators and partners.
- Build launch plans and dashboards.

### Days 61-90: Launch the fastest tests

- Sell or pre-sell selected offers.
- Track conversion, revenue, cost, contribution, workload, student participation, and satisfaction.
- Stop or redesign weak pilots quickly.
- Present a 12-month evidence-backed portfolio recommendation.

## 11. Current conclusion

Kingswood's strongest opportunity is not one large bet. It is a portfolio of mutually reinforcing initiatives that can generate revenue, employ students, improve learning, activate existing assets, and strengthen recruitment.

Near-term effort should validate summer accommodation and campus hospitality, improve current Business and MBA economics, build employer and church channels, and package short-form education and campus assets for external customers.

The 2031 vision should guide present choices without delaying small, measurable pilots. Evidence from those pilots should determine which larger structures Kingswood eventually builds.
